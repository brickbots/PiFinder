# SSD1333 brightness dims by capping rendered pixel values, not by rewriting the gray scale LUT

`DisplaySSD1333.set_brightness` drives the 1.91" panel with four settings that multiply together, not the two the SSD1351 uses. Two are registers fixing the **drive current** a lit pixel draws — per-channel **contrast** (0xC1) and **master brightness** (0xC7, scaling current by `(master + 1) / 16`). The third is the **gray scale ceiling**: the gray level that full-intensity red is rescaled onto before it reaches the panel, which fixes how *long* the pixel draws that current for. The controller's built-in linear LUT makes gray level *n* a pulse `(n - 1) * 4` DCLKs wide, so capping at level 2 is a 30x reduction in duty cycle. The fourth, adopted later, is the **pre-charge voltage** (0xBB), which scales the light a given current and duty produce and takes over below the point where lowering the ceiling further would cost the UI its dimmest shade (see the option list below).

Every constant below is measured on hardware, not derived from the datasheet.

## Why a third axis exists at all

The two current registers cannot dim this panel far enough on their own, for two compounding reasons.

Their product `contrast * (master + 1)` is always a whole number, so current-only brightness quantises to `n/16` of a contrast unit. And the panel stops emitting entirely below `contrast = 4` at `master = 0` — measured, and it stays dark however the two registers are arranged to reach the same current. So `MIN_DRIVE = 4/16` is a hard floor with **nothing reachable between `3/16` and `4/16`**.

That floor is far brighter than this display gets used at. A red night-vision panel at a dark site wants to sit near the bottom of its range; the current registers alone bottom out at about 0.16% of full, which reads as "still too bright" in the field.

Duty cycle is the way under it. Because the pixel still turns fully on and simply turns off sooner, there is no turn-on knee to fall off — gray level 2 (a 4 DCLK pulse) is still clearly visible where any comparable reduction in current is not. Measured floor: gray level 1 is dark, matching the datasheet note that level 1 has only a pre-charge stage and no current drive at all.

## Considered options

- **Cap rendered pixel values, leaving the built-in LUT alone (chosen).** luma packs red as `r & 0xF8`, so a red byte of `8n` selects gray level `n` directly. `ssd1333.gray_scale_ceiling` installs a PIL point table that rescales pixels on their way to the panel, and `display()` applies it. Clean at every level down to the floor. The rescale must work in **emitted-light space**: level *n* emits in proportion to `n - 1`, not *n*, so a pixel whose native level is *n* maps under ceiling *L* to `1 + (n - 1)(L - 1)/30`, rounded to the nearest level. Scaling the level number directly compounds that `-1` offset as the ceiling falls — mid-gray pixels (the title bar renders at value 64) dim faster than bright ones and then drop onto the dark levels 0/1 entirely, which reads as contrast changing with brightness. Field-observed: the title bar vanished between brightness settings 16 and 15 while full-intensity text dimmed smoothly on.
- **Rewrite the gray scale tables (0xB8/0xBC/0xBD) to shorten the pulses directly.** Rejected on measurement. The tables' entries must increase strictly, which floors the top level at 30 of 120 DCLKs and caps this route at ~4x — but worse, it produced visible artifacts from about 60 DCLKs down, well before that floor. The decisive comparison: a 60 DCLK pulse reached by *rewriting the table* artifacts, while the same 60 DCLK pulse reached by *capping pixel values into the stock table* is clean. So the artifacts belong to the rewritten table, not to short pulses, and the LUT stays untouched.
- **Lower the pre-charge voltage (0xBB) — now adopted as the fourth axis, for tone rather than range.** Originally shelved: the gray scale ceiling already reached the emission floor, so pre-charge bought *range* that could not be used. It was re-examined when the goal shifted to preserving tonal range, and a hardware sweep (`PiFinder.precharge_sweep`, 2026-07, by eye) found its response linear in the code, multiplicative with the other axes, artifact-free, and dark at code 0 — so the dimming multiplier is modelled as `code / 0x17`. `set_brightness` uses it only in the pre-charge regime — wherever the gray scale ceiling has already reached `MIN_TONAL_CEILING` (targets below what ceiling 5 reaches at the drive floor; settings ≤ ~24 under the knee curve): there the ceiling holds and the full pre-charge code range is searched jointly with the contrast register, whose combined grid is much denser than either register alone. Pre-charge needs at most 3x — codes 8–23 (`PRECHARGE_FLOOR`), comfortably above the 0x00–0x05 cut-out. Everywhere else it stays at the init value 0x17, where every other measured constant was calibrated. Consequence: the title bar shade now emits at *every* lit setting, and the eyeball-linear model only ever shapes the dim settings, where step-size accuracy matters least.

## Consequences

- **Dimming below `MIN_DRIVE` costs UI tonal range.** The UI's shades land on the levels *below* the ceiling, so a ceiling of 7 leaves seven distinct shades for text and chart shading. `set_brightness` therefore holds the ceiling as high as the target allows and only pulls it down once drive current alone cannot reach the target.
- **The ceiling starts at the highest value the drive floor allows and a bounded fit search trades at most `DRIVE_FIT_STEPS` steps of it away.** Starting from the ceiling whose duty cycle puts the drive right at `MIN_DRIVE` maximises tonal range, but pins the contrast register to its lowest values, where its integer steps are coarse (25% at contrast 4). The search steps the ceiling down only while the quantised registers miss the target by more than `DRIVE_FIT_TOL` (2%). The depth bound matters as much as the tolerance: unbounded, the search *prefers* very low ceilings outright, because they pair with large, finer-grained contrast values and so always fit better — surrendering exactly the tonal range the high ceiling exists to protect. (This supersedes the earlier `DIM_DRIVE = 2 * MIN_DRIVE` compromise, which bought smooth steps by halving the ceiling — and with it the number of surviving shades — at every dimmed setting.)
- **The title bar (pixel value 64) is the canary, and `MIN_TONAL_CEILING = 4` is drawn from it.** A ceiling of 4 is the lowest where a 25% shade still rounds onto an emitting level, so the fit search never trades below it — even when a lower ceiling paired with a larger contrast value would fit the brightness curve better. With pre-charge dimming carrying everything below the ceiling-4 drive floor, the title bar renders on an emitting level at every lit setting. Tests in `test_ssd1333_brightness.py` hold this line.
- **Rendering costs ~0.5 ms per frame while dimmed.** The point table is applied in `ssd1333.display`. At the full ceiling it is `None` and rendering is a true pass-through, so normal brightness pays nothing.
- **The level→brightness curve is two regimes with a soft knee at setting 20, weighted heavily toward the dim end (reshaped 2026-07 on field feedback).** The original single power law (`GAMMA = 2.5`, ~23% per keypress everywhere) spent only ~8 settings on the entire pre-charge-regime range, which read in the field as "the bottom steps change too quickly". The knee sits exactly where the pre-charge regime begins (`_KNEE_TARGET`, ceiling 4 at the drive floor). Below it, brightness rises ~6% per level — geometric per *level*, matched to the pre-charge × contrast grid spacing, so settings 1–20 walk that whole range in visible-but-gentle steps (the bottom octave's grid is ~11% coarse, so a few adjacent settings there collide; that is the panel's floor, not the curve's). Above it, brightness rises at a constant ratio per *keypress* (the UI steps the level by a percentage of itself, so this regime is a power law in the level) — about 35% per press, the unavoidable price of pinning 8.3 of the range's 9.5 log-units above the knee. Consequence for existing configs: mid-range saved settings render dimmer than before (old setting 25 ≈ new setting 50).
- **Full level is held to 70% of available current (`MAX_BRIGHTNESS`).** Measured: the top of the range blooms, bright pixels smearing into neighbours. Blooming tracks total current rather than the contrast register value — contrast 160 paired with a mid master is clean — so the cap is on brightness, and the contrast register is still free to reach its own 160 ceiling at lower master values.
- **These constants are panel measurements and do not transfer.** A different 1.91" panel, or a supplier change, invalidates `MIN_CONTRAST`, `MAX_CONTRAST`, `MAX_BRIGHTNESS` and `MIN_GRAY_SCALE_LEVEL` alike. Re-measuring means sweeping each axis on hardware and watching the screen; there is no way to derive them.
- The SSD1351 path is untouched and still passes its 0-255 level through to the two current registers.
